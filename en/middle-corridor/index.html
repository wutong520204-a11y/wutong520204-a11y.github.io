Middle Corridor: key facts

The Trans-Caspian International Transport Route (TITR), known as the Middle Corridor, links China and Central Asia with Europe without transiting Russia. Below are figures from published sources; each links to the original.

All results are estimates for planning only. Carriers, forwarders and terminals apply their own rules, rounding and tariffs — always confirm with your quote.

The route

Cargo from China travels by rail through Kazakhstan to the Caspian ports of Aktau or Kuryk, crosses the Caspian Sea to Azerbaijan, and continues through Georgia toward Türkiye or the Black Sea ports. Eastbound trains enter China via border stations such as Altynkol.

What this means for shippers

  • Westbound volumes are about four times eastbound, so eastbound (Europe/Türkiye → Central Asia/China) equipment availability and pricing can differ from westbound.
  • Winter weather on the Caspian Sea can delay sailings; the reported 12–15 day average excludes winter months.
  • Use the CBM and chargeable weight tools to prepare cargo data before requesting rail or multimodal quotes — rail groupage is often charged with a 1 m³ = 333 kg factor.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Middle Corridor?

It is the Trans-Caspian International Transport Route (TITR): rail through Kazakhstan, a Caspian Sea crossing to Azerbaijan, then onward through Georgia to Türkiye, the Black Sea and Europe.

How long does a container train take on the Middle Corridor?

According to the TITR Association, as reported by bm.ge, container trains took 12–15 days on average in 2025, excluding winter months. Door-to-door times depend on origin, destination and connections.

How much container traffic uses the route?

76,900 TEU in 2025 (+36%), and 53,574 TEU in January–August 2026 (+11% year on year), according to the sources linked above.
