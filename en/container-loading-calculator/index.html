How it works

Cartons by volume = (internal volume × utilization) ÷ carton volume. Utilization accounts for gaps, door clearance and irregular stacking; 75–90% is a common range for floor-loaded cartons.

Cartons by payload = maximum payload ÷ carton gross weight. The estimate is the lower of the two. “Simple grid stacking” shows how many cartons fit in one orientation without mixing, as a cross-check.

Internal dimensions and payload are typical values for standard steel dry containers. Real figures vary by container owner and are printed on the door; road weight limits in the origin or destination country may be lower than the container payload.

Frequently asked questions

How many CBM fit in a 20ft container?

A standard 20GP has about 33 m³ of internal volume. With cartons, about 25–28 m³ (roughly 75–85% utilization) is a realistic planning figure because of gaps and stacking.

What is the difference between 40GP and 40HQ?

Both are about 12 m long inside. The high cube (40HQ) is about 30 cm taller, giving around 76 m³ instead of about 68 m³ — useful for light, bulky cargo.

Why is my container limited by weight, not volume?

Dense goods (tiles, metal parts, liquids) reach the payload limit before the container is full. Also check road weight limits: they can be lower than the container’s rated payload.

Is this a real load plan?

No. It is an estimate. A real load plan accounts for carton orientation, stacking strength, pallets and weight distribution. Ask your forwarder or use load-planning software for final loading.
