How it works

Volumetric weight = volume × factor. Air freight with divisor 6000 means L × W × H (cm) ÷ 6000, i.e. 166.67 kg per m³. Divisor 5000 (common for express couriers) is 200 kg per m³.

Sea LCL is charged per W/M (weight or measure): 1 m³ is treated as 1,000 kg, and the higher number of freight tons is billed. Rail and road groupage often use 1 m³ = 333 kg, but the factor depends on the operator — check your tariff.

Chargeable weight = max(actual weight, volumetric weight). Air carriers typically round up to the next 0.5 kg.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between divisor 6000 and 5000?

6000 is the IATA standard for general air cargo (166.67 kg per m³). 5000 is common with express couriers and some airlines; it gives a 20% higher volumetric weight for the same box.

How is sea LCL chargeable weight calculated?

LCL is billed per W/M: the larger of the volume in m³ and the weight in metric tons. 3 m³ weighing 1,200 kg is charged as 3 W/M; 1 m³ weighing 2,500 kg is charged as 2.5 W/M.

What factor is used for rail freight from China?

Many rail groupage tariffs use 1 m³ = 333 kg (1:3), similar to European road freight, but some operators use other ratios or charge per container. Treat 333 as a planning assumption and confirm with the operator.

Is volumetric weight calculated per piece or per shipment?

Practice varies. Many carriers calculate on the total shipment, while couriers may calculate per piece and round each piece up. This tool uses the shipment total.
