FCL vs LCL cost comparison

Enter your LCL rate and FCL price to see the break-even volume and which option is cheaper for your shipment.

Also consider: FCL means less handling and no CFS consolidation wait; LCL is flexible for small, irregular volumes.

How it works

LCL cost = max(m³, tons, minimum) × rate per W/M + fixed LCL fees. FCL cost = number of containers × price per container.

Break-even W/M = (FCL price − fixed LCL fees) ÷ LCL rate. Above this volume, one full container is cheaper than LCL.

Compare like with like: include the same origin, destination and handling charges on both sides (CFS fees, deconsolidation, delivery).

Frequently asked questions

At what volume does FCL become cheaper than LCL?

It depends on rates. Divide the all-in FCL price by the all-in LCL rate per W/M. With LCL at 80 per W/M and FCL at 1,600, break-even is 20 m³. Always calculate with your own current quotes — rates change often.

What does W/M mean?

Weight or Measurement: LCL is charged on the greater of the volume in m³ or the weight in metric tons (1 t = 1 m³).

Is FCL always better above break-even?

Usually on cost, and FCL also avoids consolidation waiting time and extra handling. LCL still makes sense for small or irregular volumes, or when cash flow matters more than unit cost.
